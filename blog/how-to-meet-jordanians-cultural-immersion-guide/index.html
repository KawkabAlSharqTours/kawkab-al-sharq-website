How to Actually Meet Jordanians on Your Trip

Itinerary Guide

Most Jordan itineraries are built around sites. Our Living Jordan itinerary is deliberately light on monuments and built around people instead — cooking with an Amman family, learning a craft from a Madaba artisan, and sleeping in a Bedouin family tent rather than a tourist camp.

Cook With a Family, Not a Restaurant

A hands-on Jordanian cooking class in a family kitchen — mansaf or maqluba, cooked and eaten with the host family, not a restaurant demo. This happens on day one in Amman, setting the tone for the rest of the trip.

Learn a Craft From the Person Who Does It Professionally

A hands-on mosaic-setting class with a Madaba artisan family whose workshop has produced mosaics for churches across Jordan — you make and keep a small tile piece, guided by someone who does this as their actual trade.

Stay With a Bedouin Family in Wadi Rum

Rather than a large commercial camp, the final night joins a Bedouin family's own camp — herding goats, learning to make bread and tea over an open fire, and hearing family stories and oral poetry, with a sunrise breakfast before departure.

Visit Umm Sayhoun, Not Just Petra

A guide from the local Bdoul Bedouin community — whose families lived inside Petra until relocation to nearby Umm Sayhoun — leads the Petra portion, followed by tea with a Bdoul family and a look at traditional jewellery and embroidery-making rarely offered to day-trip groups.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is this itinerary best for?
Travelers who've already done (or don't want) a monument-heavy trip and want to leave Jordan having actually spent time with Jordanians — often a good second Jordan trip, though it works well as a first trip too.

Is it physically demanding?
No — this route involves much less walking than our Adventure or Historical itineraries. It's built around conversation and hands-on activities, not distance.
