The Secret Back-Door Entrance to Petra: Walking in Through Wadi Muthlim

Differentiator Guide

Almost every visitor to Petra walks in through the main Siq and meets the Treasury head-on, in a crowd. Wadi Muthlim is a narrow flood-control tunnel cut by the Nabataeans on Petra's northern side that does the opposite — it emerges behind the Treasury, and it's used by only a small fraction of visitors.

What Wadi Muthlim Actually Is

It's an ancient water-management tunnel, engineered by the Nabataeans to divert flash floods away from the main Siq — a serious piece of 2,000-year-old civil engineering, not just an alternative footpath. Walking through it means headlamp-lit passages and narrow rock corridors before daylight opens up behind the Treasury.

Why It Requires a Licensed Guide

The route isn't signposted or open for casual self-guided walking — it requires a licensed guide familiar with the terrain and flash-flood conditions, which is exactly why it's used by so few visitors relative to the main Siq. This isn't a way around the rules, it's a route that only functions safely with the right guide.

What the Experience Is Actually Like

Cooler, narrower, and quieter than the main Siq, with the emotional payoff arriving in reverse — instead of the Treasury slowly revealing itself ahead of you through the Siq's final turn, you emerge from the tunnel and find yourself already behind it, seeing the structure from an angle almost no other visitor experiences.

Where This Fits in a Trip

We pair Wadi Muthlim with the High Place of Sacrifice and the Wadi Farasa trail on our Rift Valley Traverse itinerary, and with a broader look at Petra's lesser-visited corners on Hidden Civilizations. It works best as a second day in Petra, after a first day covering the Treasury and Royal Tombs conventionally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wadi Muthlim difficult or dangerous?
It's a moderate walk requiring reasonable mobility and a licensed guide's judgment on conditions — it's closed to non-guided access and closed entirely after heavy rainfall due to flash-flood risk.

Can I do this instead of the main Siq, or do I need both?
Most travelers do the main Siq on their first Petra day and Wadi Muthlim on a second day — seeing both gives you the classic approach and the alternative one.
