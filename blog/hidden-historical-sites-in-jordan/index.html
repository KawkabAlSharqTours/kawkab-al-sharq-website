Beyond Jerash and Petra: 6 Hidden Historical Sites in Jordan

Itinerary Guide

Standard "historical Jordan" tours mean Jerash, Petra, and Mount Nebo. Our Hidden Civilizations itinerary keeps Petra but reframes the rest of the trip around six sites that rarely make it onto a group itinerary.

1. Qusayr Amra

A UNESCO World Heritage Umayyad bathhouse with startlingly intact 8th-century frescoes, including an early zodiac ceiling — part of a private Eastern Desert Castles loop most agencies don't run at all, alongside Qasr Kharana and Qasr Azraq.

2. Umm el-Jimal

The "Black Gem of the Desert" — an entire Byzantine and early Islamic town built from black basalt, remarkably intact and virtually unvisited.

3. Umm Qais

A Decapolis city with Roman theatres and colonnades looking out over the Sea of Galilee, the Golan Heights, and the Yarmouk Valley — one of Jordan's best views, rarely on group itineraries.

4. Iraq al-Amir

Qasr al-Abd, a 2nd-century BCE Hellenistic palace carved for the Tobiad noble family, set in a quiet valley west of Amman that most itineraries never reach.

5. Pella

An archaeological site with continuous occupation from the Neolithic through Islamic periods, excavated but lightly visited.

6. Shobak Castle

Crusader Jordan's first fortress, more remote and dramatically sited than Karak — usually a rushed 20-minute photo stop elsewhere, given proper time on our route via the King's Highway.
