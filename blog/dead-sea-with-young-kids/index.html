How to Do the Dead Sea With Young Kids (Without a Meltdown)

Family Travel

The Dead Sea's mineral water genuinely stings if it gets in eyes, cuts, or scrapes — worth knowing before you go rather than discovering mid-swim. With the right preparation, it's still one of the most memorable stops for kids on a Jordan trip.

Set Expectations Before You Arrive

Tell kids in advance that it's not a normal swim — no putting their face in the water, and the salt will sting any small cuts or scrapes. Floating (not swimming) is the whole point, and most kids find this genuinely fun once they understand what to expect.

Choose a Resort With a Real Pool Alternative

Floating alone rarely holds a young child's attention for a full day. On our Jordan for Families itinerary, we specifically book Dead Sea resorts with a water park or multiple pools alongside beach access, so the day has more than one activity.

Practical Tips

  • Rinse off in a freshwater shower immediately after floating — resorts provide these right at the beach
  • Avoid the water if your child has any open cuts or scrapes that day
  • Keep floating sessions short (10–15 minutes) for young kids — mud play and pool time can fill the rest of the visit
  • Sun protection matters more here than almost anywhere else in Jordan — the Dead Sea sits at the lowest point on Earth's land surface, with intense UV exposure

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age can kids start floating in the Dead Sea?
Most families find it manageable from around age 4–5 with close supervision; younger children can enjoy the mud and shallow water instead.

What if my child doesn't want to get in the water at all?
That's fine — the mud play and pool time at family-oriented resorts give plenty to do without floating being mandatory.
