Jordan in Winter: What to Expect in Petra and Wadi Rum

Seasonal Guide

Winter (December–February) brings cooler days (10–16°C), cold nights in Petra and Wadi Rum — occasionally near freezing — and a chance of rain or even light snow in the highlands. It's also when sites are quietest and rates drop the most of any season.

What Winter Actually Feels Like, Site by Site

Petra and Wadi Rum are comfortable by day but genuinely cold after sunset — pack a proper warm layer for evenings, especially for Wadi Rum camp nights. Machaerus's exposed hilltop on our Pilgrims' Path itinerary is cold and windy; the Dead Sea and Ma'in's hot springs, by contrast, feel especially good against cool air.

Which Itineraries Suit Winter Best

Our Desert Silence & Healing itinerary is arguably at its best in winter — warm water against cool air, and Feynan's candlelit evenings feel more atmospheric in winter's longer nights. Our Hidden Civilizations and Living Jordan routes are largely unaffected by winter weather, and sites like the Eastern Desert castles and Umm Qais are especially pleasant in cooler temperatures with far fewer other visitors around.

The One Real Weather Risk: Canyoning

Our Rift Valley Traverse itinerary's Wadi Muthlim and technical canyoning routes (Wadi Ghuweir, Khazali/Barrah scrambles) are weather-dependent in winter and may be swapped for dry desert trekking on short notice if rain is forecast — flash-flood risk closes slot canyons after heavy rainfall.

Why Winter Is the Best-Value Season

Winter Value season (December 1–February 28, excluding the December 20–January 5 holiday peak) runs 20% below standard rates across our itineraries — the deepest discount of either off-season window, alongside the quietest crowds of the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it actually snow in Jordan?
Occasional light snow is possible in the highlands (including parts of Amman and As-Salt) in winter, though it's not typical in Petra or Wadi Rum, which sit at lower elevations.

Is winter a good time for a family trip?
Yes — winter is a strong option for families avoiding summer heat with young children, though the Ma'in and Dead Sea stops are cooler for swimming. See our Jordan for Families itinerary.
