Jordan With Kids: A Complete Family Itinerary Guide (7 Days)

Itinerary Guide

Built for two adults traveling with children (roughly ages 5–15), our Jordan for Families itinerary trims walking distances, adds hands-on activities kids actually enjoy, and times outdoor stops around nap and heat windows — without rushing past Petra and Wadi Rum.

The Ajloun Zipline

A shorter, lower children's line (from age 6 / 120cm) runs alongside the main 330m adult line, so parents and kids can ride together — consistently one of the most popular stops on this route.

Ma'in's Swimmable Waterfalls

Unlike a standard hot-spring soak, kids can swim directly under Hammamat Ma'in's warm waterfalls — far more interactive, and a reliable hit even with reluctant younger travelers.

Petra as a Treasure Hunt, Not a Lecture

A guide-led trail using a kid-oriented spotting sheet — identifying carved urns, tomb facades, and Nabataean channel work — rather than standard commentary, plus a horse-drawn carriage option for the first stretch of the Siq.

A Family-Friendly Wadi Rum Camp

Proper toilets and showers, a shorter activity-based jeep tour (dune running, a camel ride), and a stargazing session pitched for kids — constellation stories rather than technical astronomy.

Child Pricing

Children 2–5 are priced at approximately 35% of the adult rate; children 6–11 at approximately 50%. Children under 12 enter Petra free and receive free or half-price entry at most other sites on this route.

Frequently Asked Questions

What age range does this suit best?
Roughly 5–15. Younger children can still travel, but some activities (the Monastery climb, longer treks) are optional and easily skipped.

Is Petra actually manageable with kids?
Yes, with the pacing this itinerary uses. See our dedicated post, Is Petra Hard for Kids? A Parent's Honest Guide, for more detail.
