Planning a School Trip to Jordan: What Educators Need to Know

Group Travel

Jordan is a genuinely strong destination for curriculum-linked school and university trips — Petra and Jerash for archaeology and world history, Dana and the Dead Sea for environmental science and geology. Here's what to plan for beyond the sightseeing.

Chaperone Ratios and Group Size

Our standard is 1 adult chaperone per 10 students for secondary school groups; university groups typically travel with 1 faculty lead per 20 students plus a dedicated group coordinator from our side. Group sizes of 15–45 students are standard, with larger cohorts split into parallel groups on synchronized schedules.

Safeguarding and Risk Management

All drivers and guides are subject to background verification for school groups, and a written risk assessment and emergency protocol is provided to the school or university in advance. Comprehensive group medical/travel insurance is arranged centrally where required by the institution, rather than left to individual students.

Curriculum-Linked Tracks

We run two dedicated 6-day/5-night tracks: a History & Archaeology focus (Jordan Museum, Jerash, Petra's Nabataean water engineering, Wadi Rum petroglyphs) and an Environmental Science & Ecology focus (Dana biodiversity, Feynan's sustainability systems, Dead Sea geology). Both include specialist workshop sessions, not just guided sightseeing.

What's Included

Twin/triple-share accommodation, daily breakfast, 4 dinners, a specialist educational guide throughout, coach transport, all entrance fees plus one specialist workshop per day, and a risk assessment document with 24/7 emergency contact. One complimentary place is provided per 15 paying students, the standard industry ratio for chaperone comps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a school trip cost per student?
From approximately 445 JD (≈ $630) per student for either track, group of 20+, twin/triple share. A version with 2 additional guest specialist lectures from local university faculty runs approximately 495 JD.

How far in advance should we book?
School and university trips typically involve longer planning cycles than leisure travel — we recommend starting the conversation at least 6 months ahead, especially for larger cohorts.

Planning a School or University Trip?

We'll send a full proposal package — pricing, risk assessment, and chaperone details — quickly so you can compare options with your committee.
