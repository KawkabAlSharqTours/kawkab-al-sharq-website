Umm Qais: The Jordan Viewpoint Overlooking Three Countries

Differentiator Guide

Umm Qais, ancient Gadara, is a Decapolis city with Roman theatres and colonnades — and one of the best views in Jordan, looking out over the Sea of Galilee, the Golan Heights, and the Yarmouk Valley simultaneously. It rarely appears on group itineraries.

What Umm Qais Is

One of the ten cities of the Decapolis, a loose federation of Greco-Roman cities in the region. Its ruins include a well-preserved Roman theatre built from black basalt, colonnaded streets, and a mausoleum — substantial enough to spend a couple of unhurried hours.

The View Itself

From the site's edge, you can see three distinct territories at once — a genuinely striking geography lesson as much as a historical one. This kind of panoramic context is rare at Jordan's other Roman sites.

Religious Significance

Umm Qais is traditionally associated with the New Testament account of the exorcism of the Gadarene demoniac, which is why it also appears on our Pilgrims' Path itinerary alongside its historical significance on Hidden Civilizations.

Why It's Overlooked

Umm Qais sits in Jordan's far north, off the standard Amman-Petra-Wadi Rum corridor most tours run — geographically inconvenient for a quick add-on, which is exactly why competitors skip it despite its significance.
