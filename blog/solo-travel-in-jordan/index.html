Solo Travel in Jordan: What You Should Know

Practical Guide

Jordan is a genuinely solo-friendly destination — a well-established tourism infrastructure, widely spoken English in tourist areas, and a strong culture of hospitality. For broader safety context, see our post on whether Jordan is safe to visit; this post focuses on what's specific to traveling alone.

Joining a Small Group vs a Fully Private Trip

Solo travelers on our itineraries can either join a small group (our routes cap at 10 travelers) or book privately. Joining a small group is often the better value and a natural way to meet other travelers; a private trip offers full schedule flexibility at a higher per-person cost.

Single Supplement Costs

Most of our itineraries carry a single supplement — typically 75–165 JD depending on the route and number of nights — to cover the cost of a private room rather than twin-share. This is standard across the industry, not unique to us, and it's clearly listed on every itinerary page.

Practical Solo Travel Tips

  • Share your itinerary and daily check-ins with someone at home, as you would anywhere
  • A private vehicle and driver (included in most of our itineraries) removes most of the logistical friction solo travelers face with public transport
  • Evenings in Wadi Rum camps and smaller guesthouses are genuinely social — solo travelers often end up in conversation with other guests over dinner

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Jordan a good destination for solo female travelers?
Many solo female travelers visit Jordan successfully. As with solo travel anywhere, standard precautions apply — dress modestly in conservative areas, use licensed transport, and avoid walking alone late at night in unfamiliar areas.

Can I avoid the single supplement by joining a group?
Joining our small-group departures doesn't waive the single supplement for a private room, but it does let you share the overall trip experience with other travelers at no extra cost.
