A Night With a Bedouin Family: What a Wadi Rum Homestay Is Really Like

Differentiator Guide

Most Wadi Rum visits mean a jeep tour and a night at a commercial tourist camp. On our Living Jordan itinerary, the final night is different — guests join a Bedouin family's own camp, not a camp built for tourists.

What Actually Happens

The afternoon involves herding goats alongside the family, learning to make bread and tea over an open fire, and hearing family stories and oral poetry passed down through generations. It's unstructured in a way a commercial camp's schedule isn't — conversation and daily life, not a program of activities.

How It's Different From a Commercial Camp

A commercial Wadi Rum camp, even a good one, is built and staffed for tourists. This is the family's actual home base — accommodation is genuinely shared rather than a private tourist tent, and the evening centers on the family rather than a curated performance of Bedouin culture.

What to Expect Practically

Shared, simple accommodation rather than an en-suite private tent — this is the trade-off for authenticity. Sunrise breakfast with the family the next morning is included before transfer to Aqaba.

Who This Suits

Travelers who are comfortable with simpler accommodation in exchange for a genuine cultural exchange, rather than travelers prioritizing privacy and amenities — for the latter, our other itineraries offer private en-suite Wadi Rum camps instead. See our comparison of shared vs. private Wadi Rum camps to decide which fits you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this suitable for all travelers?
It suits travelers comfortable with simple, shared accommodation and an unscripted evening. If privacy and amenities matter more to you, our other itineraries offer private Wadi Rum camps.

Which itinerary includes this experience?
Our Living Jordan itinerary, as the closing night before departure via Aqaba.

Want This Experience?

A Bedouin family camp night is included on our Living Jordan itinerary.
